## Our Pledge

Contribution Ledger is built on a simple idea: **people should be recognized for what they contribute, not for what they own, buy, or shout loudest.**

We apply the same principle to our community. Here, contribution matters more than status, money, credentials, or volume. A thoughtful critique is worth more than a hundred "+1"s. A careful review is worth more than a flashy PR.

We pledge to make participation in this project a respectful, harassment-free experience for everyone — regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We do not pledge to be neutral about ideas. **We pledge to be respectful to people while being rigorous about ideas.**

## 1. Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

### 1.1. Be respectful

- Disagree with ideas, not with people.
- Critique the argument, not the author.
- Assume good faith until proven otherwise.
- Acknowledge when you are wrong.

### 1.2. Be clear

- Say what you mean.
- Explain your reasoning.
- Define terms before using them.
- Cite sources when you reference them.

### 1.3. Be constructive

- If you criticize, propose an alternative.
- If you find a problem, suggest a fix.
- If you disagree, say what would change your mind.
- If you do not know, say so.

### 1.4. Be patient

- Not everyone speaks English as a first language.
- Not everyone has the same background.
- Not everyone has read every document.
- Repeat yourself if needed. Kindly.

### 1.5. Be honest

- Do not pretend to know what you do not.
- Do not take credit for others' work.
- Do not hide conflicts of interest.
- Do not promise what you cannot deliver.

### 1.6. Be open

- Welcome newcomers.
- Answer questions, even basic ones.
- Share what you learned.
- Help others contribute.

### 1.7. Be focused

- Stay on topic.
- Keep discussions in the right place.
- Avoid repeating arguments already settled.
- Move long debates to a dedicated thread.

## 2. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are unacceptable in any project space.

### 2.1. Harassment

- Insults, slurs, or derogatory comments.
- Personal or political attacks.
- Threats of violence or harm.
- Stalking, following, or persistent unwanted contact.
- Sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Deliberate misgendering or use of rejected names.
- Publishing others' private information without consent (doxxing).

### 2.2. Discrimination

- Exclusion based on identity.
- Jokes or comments that demean a group.
- Denying someone's experience.
- Treating people differently based on who they are.

### 2.3. Disruption

- Deliberately derailing discussions.
- Spamming, flooding, or repeating the same point.
- Trolling — provoking reactions for amusement.
- Sea-lioning — asking questions in bad faith.
- Refusing to engage with counterarguments while continuing to argue.
- Repeatedly reopening settled decisions without new information.

### 2.4. Dishonesty

- Plagiarism.
- Fabricating evidence or citations.
- Misrepresenting others' positions (strawmanning).
- Editing quotes to change their meaning.
- Claiming endorsement you do not have.
- Using AI-generated content without disclosure when it matters.

### 2.5. Abuse of process

- Opening multiple issues for the same point.
- Filing PRs that ignore prior feedback.
- Using the reporting system to silence critics.
- Coordinating to overwhelm a discussion with volume.
- Impersonating a maintainer or contributor.

## 3. Project-Specific Rules

Contribution Ledger has a specific mission. Some behaviors are unacceptable **because they contradict what the project stands for**, not because they are universally wrong.

### 3.1. No crypto shilling

CL is explicitly **not a cryptocurrency**. Do not use project spaces to:

- Promote tokens, coins, or NFTs.
- Pitch "adding a token" as a solution to anything.
- Suggest CL should "pump", "moon", or "go to the moon".
- Promote exchanges, DEXs, or yield schemes.
- Suggest the project needs a "fair launch", "presale", or "airdrop".

You may criticize CL's anti-token stance — that is welcome. You may not use the project as a stage for crypto promotion.

### 3.2. No speculation disguised as feedback

Feedback like "this would be more popular if people could buy CU" is not feedback — it is a proposal to destroy the project's core principle. Such suggestions will be closed with a link to [Section 11 of CONTRIBUTING.md](./CONTRIBUTING.md#11-what-we-will-not-accept).

You may argue the principle is wrong. You may not ignore it.

### 3.3. No hype

CL is at the whitepaper stage. Do not:

- Claim the project will "replace Bitcoin", "kill GitHub", or "revolutionize X".
- Promise features that are not designed.
- Announce timelines on behalf of the project.
- Speak for the project publicly without being authorized.

Enthusiastic support is welcome. Misrepresentation is not.

### 3.4. No recruitment for unrelated projects

Do not use CL spaces to recruit for:

- Your own token.
- Your own DAO.
- Your own platform.
- Your political movement.
- Your paid services.

Exception: if your project is genuinely related (e.g., an open-source tool that helps CL), ask a maintainer first.

### 3.5. No monetization of participation

Do not:

- Sell access to CL contributors.
- Charge for introductions.
- Run paid promotions for the project.
- Use CL reputation (once live) as a paid endorsement.

### 3.6. Respect the boundary between criticism and sabotage

Criticism is welcome — even harsh criticism. Sabotage is not.

**Criticism:** "The reputation formula in Section 9.2 seems gameable because X."
**Sabotage:** "This project is dead. Nobody should contribute. Everyone leave."

If you believe the project is flawed, say so clearly and with reasons. If you believe it should not exist, that is a valid position — state it once, then leave the space to those who want to build it.

## 4. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to:

- The GitHub repository and all its spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, wiki).
- Official chat channels (Telegram, Matrix, Discord — once created).
- Official calls and meetings.
- Project events, online or offline.
- Any space where you are officially representing the project.

It also applies **outside** these spaces when your behavior directly affects the project's ability to function — for example, harassing a contributor on social media after a disagreement here.

This Code of Conduct does **not** govern your private conversations, your other projects, or your opinions. It governs your behavior **as a participant in this project.**

## 5. Enforcement Responsibilities

### 5.1. Maintainers

Maintainers are responsible for:

- Clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct.
- Taking fair and consistent action when violations occur.
- Removing, editing, or rejecting comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that violate this Code.
- Communicating reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.
- Protecting the privacy and safety of reporters.

Maintainers who do not enforce this Code in good faith may be removed from their role by the same process that applies to any participant.

### 5.2. What maintainers are not

Maintainers are not:

- Judges of your character outside the project.
- Arbiters of your political views.
- Responsible for your feelings about technical disagreement.
- Required to respond to every message.
- Required to justify every moderation decision in public.

Maintainers are humans with limited time. They will make mistakes. You can appeal.

## 6. Reporting

### 6.1. How to report

If you experience or witness a violation of this Code of Conduct, report it to:

**Email:** [contact email — to be added]  
**Subject line:** `CL Code of Conduct Report`

Include:

- What happened.
- Where and when.
- Who was involved.
- Any evidence (links, screenshots, quotes).
- Whether you want to be contacted for follow-up.
- Whether you want the report to be public or private.

You may also contact any maintainer privately if the report involves another maintainer.

### 6.2. What happens next

1. **Acknowledgment** within 72 hours.
2. **Review** by maintainers not involved in the incident.
3. **Decision** within 14 days, unless the case is complex.
4. **Action** taken according to Section 7.
5. **Follow-up** with the reporter on the outcome, within privacy limits.

### 6.3. Confidentiality

Reports are handled confidentially. We will not share your identity without your consent, except where required by law or where necessary to protect others.

### 6.4. Good faith

Reports must be made in good faith. Knowingly false reports are themselves a violation of this Code.

Disagreeing with someone is not harassment. Being criticized is not harassment. Being told your PR will not be merged is not harassment. Harassment is a pattern of behavior targeting a person.

## 7. Enforcement Guidelines

Enforcement is graduated. The goal is to correct behavior, not to punish people. But repeated or severe violations lead to permanent removal.

### 7.1. Correction

**Used for:** minor, first-time issues — a harsh comment, an off-topic post, a misunderstood joke.

**Action:** A private message from a maintainer explaining what was wrong and what is expected. No public record.

**Duration:** None.

### 7.2. Warning

**Used for:** repeated minor issues, or a single more serious one — personal attacks, deliberate derailment, ignoring feedback.

**Action:** A formal warning, with a link to this Code. May include a temporary restriction on posting in a specific thread or channel.

**Duration:** Up to 7 days.

### 7.3. Temporary ban

**Used for:** a serious violation, or a pattern of behavior after warnings — harassment, discrimination, sabotage, repeated bad-faith engagement.

**Action:** Temporary removal from all project spaces.

**Duration:** 7–90 days, depending on severity.

### 7.4. Permanent ban

**Used for:** severe violations — threats, doxxing, coordinated harassment, fraud, plagiarism at scale, repeated violations after temporary bans.

**Action:** Permanent removal from all project spaces. Contributions may be reverted or excluded. The participant's name may be listed in a public moderation log (with details kept minimal to protect privacy).

**Duration:** Permanent.

### 7.5. Content removal

Violating content may be:

- Edited to remove the violation.
- Hidden without deletion (where possible).
- Deleted.

Reasons will be given when appropriate.

### 7.6. Restoration

Bans may be lifted early if:

- The person acknowledges the behavior.
- The person takes steps to avoid repetition.
- The maintainers agree that the risk is low.

There is no entitlement to restoration. It is a judgment call.

## 8. Appeals

### 8.1. Who can appeal

Any participant who receives a warning, temporary ban, or permanent ban may appeal.

### 8.2. How to appeal

Send an email to [contact email] with:

- Subject: `CL Code of Conduct Appeal`
- The original decision and date.
- Why you believe the decision was wrong.
- What you would do differently.

### 8.3. Who reviews the appeal

Appeals are reviewed by maintainers who were **not** involved in the original decision. If no such maintainer exists (small team), an external reviewer may be invited.

### 8.4. Timeline

- Acknowledgment within 7 days.
- Decision within 30 days.
- The decision is final for the given incident.

### 8.5. Limits

- One appeal per incident.
- Appeals must be made in good faith.
- Abusive appeals may themselves be a violation.

## Summary
